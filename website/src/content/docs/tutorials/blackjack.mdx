You will walk through `examples/blackjack`: up to five players, each playing their own hand
against the house. This tutorial assumes you finished [tic-tac-toe](/tutorials/tic-tac-toe/)
and the [quiz](/tutorials/quiz/), so `defineGame`, actions, `reject()` and `view()` are not
explained again. Here you meet cards, phases that hand over to each other, turn timers and
money.

## A hand in three phases

A hand moves through three phases, declared under `phases` in one `defineGame` call:

1. `betting`: everyone bets at once, against a timer (a round, as in the quiz).
2. `play`: players take turns to hit, stand or double.
3. `dealer`: the dealer plays, the hands are settled and the next hand is scheduled.

Each phase has its own actions, so `hit` simply does not exist while people are betting.
`ctx.phase.go('play')` moves on. See [phases](/how-to/phases/) for the full model.

## Cards and shuffling

`shoehive/cards` has the card helpers. A `Card` is a two-character string such as `'AS'` or
`'TD'`. `shoe(6)` is an unshuffled stack of six decks, and `blackjackValue(cards)` scores a hand
as `{ total, soft, blackjack, bust }`.

Shuffle with `ctx.random`, never `Math.random()`. It is the table's seeded generator, so a
replay or a test with a fixed seed deals the same cards. The `deal` code runs when the `play`
phase starts:

<Snippet file="examples/blackjack/src/game.ts" region="deal" title="src/game.ts (play phase)" />

The shoe lives in `state`, so it is saved and replayed with the table. It is also secret,
which the view below takes care of.

## Betting: a round and a wallet

The `betting` phase is a round with `closeWhen: 'all-submitted'` and a 20 second limit.
`bet` is a `once` action, so each seat bets once. When the round closes, `onClose` starts the
deal if anyone bet, or opens a new round if nobody did.

<Snippet
  file="examples/blackjack/src/game.ts"
  region="betting"
  title="src/game.ts (betting phase)"
/>

The bet is money, so it goes through `ctx.wallet` (see
[wallets and real-money safety](/how-to/wallets-and-real-money-safety/) and
[ADR 0007](/contributing/adr/0007-wallet-ledger-semantics/)):

- `await ctx.wallet.reserve(seat, amount, key)` takes the amount out of the player's
  available balance and returns `true`, or `false` when they cannot afford it. It runs
  immediately because the action needs the answer. Here a `false` becomes
  `reject('INSUFFICIENT_FUNDS')`, so the whole action is undone.
- `commit(key)`, `release(key)` and `credit(seat, amount, key)` do not return anything.
  They are held back and sent only after the action has been saved, so money never moves for
  a state change that did not happen.
- The key names the reservation. It must be unique at the table, so the example builds it
  from the hand and seat number: one key per hand per seat.

<Snippet file="examples/blackjack/src/game.ts" region="keys" title="src/game.ts" />

`double` reserves the same stake again, under its own key. Amounts are whole numbers (chips,
or cents), never fractions.

## Turns, skipping and the turn timer

The `play` phase declares who may act and what happens to a slow player:

<Snippet file="examples/blackjack/src/game.ts" region="turns" title="src/game.ts (play phase)" />

- Turns go round the seats in order. `skip` passes over seats with no hand, and over hands
  that are already done (a natural blackjack, or a stand). Both `hit` and `stand` end by
  calling `ctx.turn.next()`, and when no seat is left they call `ctx.phase.go('dealer')`.
- `timeoutMs` arms a 30 second timer each time the turn changes. When it fires, Shoehive runs
  the action named in `onTimeout` with `{ seat }` as input.
- That action must exist, and it should be `internal`: it cannot be sent by a client, only by
  a timer or by your own code. Otherwise any player could stand for somebody else.

<Snippet
  file="examples/blackjack/src/game.ts"
  region="autostand"
  title="src/game.ts (play phase actions)"
/>

For other clocks see [timers and turn clocks](/how-to/timers-and-turn-clocks/). The dealer
phase uses one more, `ctx.timers.after('nextHand', NEXT_HAND_MS, 'nextHand')`, which runs the
internal `nextHand` action five seconds later to start betting again.

## The dealer and settlement

When the dealer phase starts, the dealer draws to 17, then every hand is scored. For each
hand the code commits the reservations (the stake is now really spent) and credits the
winnings, which include the stake back on a win or a push:

<Snippet file="examples/blackjack/src/game.ts" region="dealer" title="src/game.ts (dealer phase)" />

Net effect for a 100 chip bet that wins: 100 reserved at the bet, committed at settlement,
200 credited. A loss credits nothing. Because the credit key is `pay:<hand>:<seat>`, it
cannot pay twice for one hand even if something retries.

## Leaving mid-hand

A player can stand up at any time, and their money must not get stuck. `onStand` handles it:
before the deal (no cards yet) the bet is released, so it returns to their balance. After the
deal the bet is forfeit, so it is committed.

<Snippet file="examples/blackjack/src/game.ts" region="leave" title="src/game.ts" />

`onSit` copies the wallet balance into `state.bankroll`. That number is only for display; the
wallet stays the source of truth.

## Hiding the hole card

The dealer's second card must stay face down until the dealer plays. The view builds what
each client may see, and `hideCard` turns a card into `null`:

<Snippet file="examples/blackjack/src/game.ts" region="view" title="src/game.ts" />

Two details matter. The dealer's total is `null` until the reveal, so the total cannot give
the card away. And the shoe is sent as a count only, so nobody can read the next card.
Clients receive views, never `state`, so there is nothing to peek at. More patterns in
[hidden information](/how-to/hidden-information/).

## Run it with a wallet

A game that calls `ctx.wallet` needs a wallet on the server. Without one, every call fails
with `WALLET_NOT_CONFIGURED`. `MemoryWallet` is an in-memory ledger for development:

<Snippet file="examples/blackjack/src/server.ts" region="server" title="src/server.ts" />

Every new player starts with 1000. For real money you implement the `Wallet` interface on
your own database or payment service, and the rest of the game does not change. Shoehive is
bookkeeping, not a licensed gaming system: read
[wallets and real-money safety](/how-to/wallets-and-real-money-safety/) first.
